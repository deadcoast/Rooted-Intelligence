
This exploration builds upon several converging streams of research across AI, cognitive science, philosophy of mind, and mathematics. Key influences and related work include:

## Vector Semantics and Distributional Representations

The conceptual foundation of this work extends research in distributional semantics pioneered by Landauer and Dumais (1997) through their Latent Semantic Analysis[[2.pdf|[2] ]], further developed by Mikolov et al. (2013) with Word2Vec[[3.pdf|[3] ]] and Pennington et al. (2014) with GloVe[[4.pdf|[4] ]]. These approaches established that semantic meaning could be captured through statistical relationships in high-dimensional spaces.

## Transformer Architectures and Attention Mechanisms

The technical analysis draws heavily on Ashish Vaswani et al.'s (2017) "Attention is All You Need,"[[5.pdf|[5] ]] which introduced the transformer architecture that enables modern LLMs. Subsequent work by Jacob Devlin et al. (2019) on BERT[[9.pdf|[9] ]] and Tom B Brown et al. (2020) on GPT-3[[10.pdf|[10] ]] demonstrated how these architectures scale to produce increasingly sophisticated language capabilities.

## Geometric Deep Learning

Bronstein et al.'s (2021) "Geometric Deep Learning"[[11.pdf|[11] ]] framework provides essential context for understanding neural networks through mathematical structures and symmetries. This perspective aligns with my analysis of AI cognition as fundamentally geometric rather than symbolic.

## Philosophy of AI and Alternative Cognition

This work engages with philosophical perspectives on non-human intelligence, including Clark and Chalmers' (1998) extended mind thesis[[12.pdf|[12] ]] and Daniel Dennett's recent work on competence without comprehension[[13.pdf|[13] ]]. Shanahan's (2015) discussions of consciousness and AI provide essential counterpoints to purely functionalist accounts of machine intelligence[[14.pdf|[14] ]].

## Topological Data Analysis

The mathematical frameworks employed are related to research in topological data analysis, particularly the work of Zomorodian and Carlsson (2005) on computing persistent homology[[15.pdf|[15] ]]. These approaches inform my analysis of how meaning might emerge from the topological features of data spaces.

## Information Theory and Complexity

Information-theoretic perspectives, especially the Kolmogorov complexity[[16.pdf|[16] ]] and minimum description length principles developed by Rissanen (1978)[[17.pdf|[17] ]] , underpin the discussion of semantic compression and representation. This aligns with more recent work on information bottlenecks in deep learning by Tishby and Zaslavsky (2015)[[18.pdf|[18] ]].

My work differs from these precedents by synthesizing technical and philosophical perspectives into a unified framework specifically focused on understanding the "internal language" of AI systems. While much research has focused on improving AI performance or drawing analogies to human cognition, fewer studies have explored the alternative cognitive architecture that emerges from vector space operations as a legitimate form of intelligence in its own right.
